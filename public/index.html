Arnold Tongue Lab

Flicker a light at a 10 Hz alpha rhythm and compare two explanations of the SSVEP. Entrainment: the flicker pulls a noisy alpha oscillator into step. Superposition: a fixed evoked response simply adds to alpha that keeps running on its own. Both are scored the way the paper does it, from the EEG-minus-flicker phase difference and its normalized Shannon entropy ρ.

Things to try

  1. Start at the tongue border, the default. The Adler oscillator holds a plateau, slips one cycle, then locks again. That is the paper's intermittency.
  2. Switch to Superposition and change nothing else. Plateaus and slips are still there, because alpha waxes and wanes against a fixed evoked response. The map also forms a wedge.
  3. Set amplitude waxing and waning to 0. In Superposition, slips become regular or disappear. In Entrainment they come from the dynamics and stay.
  4. Compare the spectra. Entrainment gives one peak, pulled to the flicker frequency. Superposition gives two: the flicker at f and alpha still at 10 Hz.
  5. Raise the phase noise under Entrainment. The tongue edges blur and slips appear even inside it.

The two models

Entrainment (noisy Adler oscillator)
dφ/dt = ω₀ + K·sin(ωt − φ) + √(2D)·ξ(t)
K = 2π · κ · L
locks when |f − IAF| ≤ κ · L
EEG = a(t)·e^{iφ} + noise

Superposition (evoked + independent alpha)
EEG = Y·e^{i(ωt+δ)} + a(t)·e^{iφα} + noise
dφα/dt = ω₀ + √(2D)·ξ(t)
Y = g · L / √(1 + (Δf / w)²)

Both
Δphase = arg(EEG) − ωt
ρ = (ln 80 − S) / ln 80

What this leaves out

The models are simplified on purpose. They use the analytic signal directly instead of the paper's FIR filter and Hilbert transform. Coupling is sinusoidal rather than the per-flash phase response of real light pulses. There is one oscillator and one channel.

Parameters are chosen to make the behaviour visible, not fitted to the paper's data. Plateaus are detected as |slope| < 0.3 cycles/s over 0.5 s, lasting at least 0.5 s. Like the paper's threshold, that cut-off is arbitrary.

ρ has a small positive floor from finite samples, so a value near 0.01 means no locking.
